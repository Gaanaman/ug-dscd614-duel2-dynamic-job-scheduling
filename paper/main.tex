\title{Dynamic Job Scheduling with a Dueling Deep Q-Network}

\author{Kyeremeh Faithful\\22424515\and Daniel K. Adotey\\22424924\and Caleb Abakah Mensah\\22424188}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Dispatching rules answer the scheduling question with one greedy criterion applied identically in
every state, and cannot trade a small loss now against a larger saving later. We formulate dynamic
job scheduling on five heterogeneous parallel machines with Poisson arrivals as a masked-action
Markov decision process and train a Dueling Deep Q-Network on it under two action formulations:
direct assignment of a queued job to a machine, and selection among eight dispatching rules. On
thirty held-out instances across three seeds, the rule formulation with three-step returns reaches
a return of $-1.235 \pm 0.025$, beating First-Come-First-Served, Shortest-Job-First and Round Robin
on every metric by margins exceeding the seed spread. It does not beat the strongest rule in its own
action set, Apparent Tardiness Cost at $-1.125$. An ablation shows three-step returns contribute a
real improvement while prioritised experience replay contributes nothing measurable, which runs
against the recipe of the closest published precedent. We report the shortfall, locate it in the
flat state encoding rather than in compute or reward design, and give the measurements that rule
out the alternatives.
\end{abstract}
