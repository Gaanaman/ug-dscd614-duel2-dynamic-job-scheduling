\title{Dynamic Job Scheduling with a Dueling Deep Q-Network}

\author{Kyeremeh Faithful\\22424515\and Daniel K. Adotey\\22424924\and Caleb Abakah Mensah\\22424188}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Dispatching rules apply one greedy criterion identically in every state, and cannot trade a
small loss now against a larger saving later. We formulate dynamic
job scheduling on five heterogeneous parallel machines with Poisson arrivals as a masked-action
Markov decision process and train a Dueling Deep Q-Network on it under two action formulations:
direct assignment of a queued job to a machine, and selection among eight dispatching rules. On
thirty held-out instances across three seeds, the rule formulation with three-step returns reaches
a return of $-1.235 \pm 0.025$, beating First-Come-First-Served and Round Robin on every metric, and Shortest-Job-First on
return, weighted tardiness and makespan, by margins exceeding the seed spread; Shortest-Job-First
retains the lower average waiting time and fewer missed deadlines. It scores below the strongest rule in its own action set, Apparent Tardiness Cost at $-1.125$. An ablation shows that three-step returns improve the
return while prioritised experience replay leaves the return unchanged within the seed spread. The
difference from ATC is traced to the flat state encoding, with compute and reward design excluded
by measurement.
\end{abstract}
